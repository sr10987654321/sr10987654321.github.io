Toolkit 3:
Powerful Movement Verbs

Make the danger move.

Learning intention

Intensify the danger using powerful movement verbs.

Success criteria · I can…

  • choose powerful verbs to describe how the cat moves.
  • use movement verbs that make the cat seem dangerous.
  • choose verbs that show how the danger is increasing.
Picture prompt · look closely

What's happening in this alley?

Talk with your partner

  • What can you see?
  • Where is the danger?
  • How might the creature move?
  • Which movement would be the most frightening?

Important: Describe what the threat does, not just how the cat feels.

Zoom in on the writer's choices

Find the movement verbs.

“A vague shape slipped into a doorway.”

“...an enormous cat paced through the darkness, slinking along the rain-washed pavements.”

Slipped feels quiet and mysterious. Paced feels deliberate. Slinking suggests secretive, stealthy movement.

Build danger in three steps

Don't go straight to the pounce.

1 · A clue

It slipped into the shadows.

2 · Closer

It stalked towards the lorry.

3 · Danger!

It lunged from the darkness.

Read these aloud. How does the danger build?

Your turn · in your book

The approaching threat

Write three to five connected sentences about the creature or mysterious figure moving closer.

  • Use at least three precise movement verbs.
  • Build the danger gradually: clue → approach → danger.
  • Make the reader want to know what happens next.

Extension: Add Toolkit 1 and Toolkit 2 without taking attention away from today's movement verbs.

Teacher example · reveal

How does the threat move?

A shadow slipped through the doorway. Slowly, the dark figure stalked across the cobblestones. The collie's paws trembled. Had it seen her? Without warning, the figure lunged from the steps.

Underline each movement verb and discuss how the danger increases.
