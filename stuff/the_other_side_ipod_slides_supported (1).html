What if the person we think is being mean… isn’t?

PAUSE / QUESTION / CHOOSE01
TEACHER FIELD GUIDE · 01/20

Set the central dilemma

ASK THE CLASS

Ask: Have you ever been sure someone was wrong, then later discovered you missed something? Students can think silently; do not invite names or specific classroom allegations.

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

Today is not about letting unkind behaviour go. It is about avoiding unfair accusations, protecting people who need help and taking responsibility for our own response.

FOLLOW-UP / TEACHER NOTE

A useful framing: “I can care about someone who feels hurt without deciding another person must be bad.”

PAUSE / QUESTION / CHOOSE05
TEACHER FIELD GUIDE · 05/20

Observation versus interpretation

ASK THE CLASS

Ask: Can we prove who owns the biscuits just by seeing the two people eat them? What detail have we assumed?

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

Observation: both characters take biscuits from a packet between them. Inference: one person owns the packet and the other is stealing. We have not yet established ownership.

FOLLOW-UP / TEACHER NOTE

Model language: “I noticed…” versus “I think…” or “Maybe…”.

Unpack the twist

ASK THE CLASS

After the ending, ask: What new information changed our interpretation? Did the boy suddenly become kind, or was he already being kind?

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

The woman discovers her own biscuits. The boy had been sharing his biscuits. The actions we saw remain the same; their meaning changes with the missing information.

FOLLOW-UP / TEACHER NOTE

Key takeaway: Being confident about an explanation is not the same as knowing it is true.

The important difference

GOOD
INTENTIONS.
UNFAIR
ACTIONS.

Sometimes we think we’re defending a friend or doing the right thing—but we can still hurt someone by blaming, insulting or spreading the story.

Wanting to help does not make every response helpful.
PAUSE / QUESTION / CHOOSE07
TEACHER FIELD GUIDE · 07/20

Name the turning point

ASK THE CLASS

Ask: At what moment did the woman stop simply feeling annoyed and begin treating the boy unfairly?

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

Her belief that the boy was taking from her influenced her glare, irritation and behaviour. Her feelings were understandable given her assumption, but her response still affected someone else.

FOLLOW-UP / TEACHER NOTE

Avoid saying emotions are bad. Feelings give us information; actions are choices.

Helping vs taking sides

ATTACKING.

HELPING

Check whether someone is okay.

Ask calm questions.

Get adult help when needed.

TAKING SIDES UNFAIRLY

Reveal the context

ASK THE CLASS

Ask: Does learning about Oliver change how we understand Mia? What could Jack do now to repair the harm?

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

Mia was returning the ball to its owner. Jack can correct his claim, apologise to Mia, and stop other children repeating it.

FOLLOW-UP / TEACHER NOTE

Discuss: Even if Mia had been wrong, insults and spreading rumours would not be a fair response.

THE OTHER SIDE OF THE STORYYEAR 4 HEALTH / 02
When should you tell an adult?

REPORTING
IS NOT
GOSSIP.

If someone is hurt, bullied, threatened or unsafe, tell a trusted adult. You do not have to investigate everything yourself.

Describe what you actually saw or heard. Be clear about what you are unsure of.
PAUSE / QUESTION / CHOOSE12
TEACHER FIELD GUIDE · 12/20

Make reporting safe

ASK THE CLASS

Ask: How is telling a teacher about a safety concern different from telling classmates someone is horrible?

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

Reporting seeks help and shares observations accurately. Gossip spreads claims, often without verification, to people who cannot help. If someone is hurt or unsafe, seek adult help straight away.

FOLLOW-UP / TEACHER NOTE

Useful script: “I saw X. I’m worried about Y. I’m not sure what happened before that.”

Model better language

ASK THE CLASS

Students rewrite one accusation into a neutral, helpful statement. Ask pairs to perform both versions.

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

Case 1: the silent friend

ASK THE CLASS

Before reveal ask: What do we know? Name two explanations other than deliberate ignoring. What can Noah say?

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

Possible explanations: Zara did not notice, was distracted, was upset, or needed space. Fair response: “Hey, are you okay? I waved earlier but maybe you didn’t see.”

FOLLOW-UP / TEACHER NOTE

Reveal the extra information only after predictions, then compare interpretations.

Group chat dilemma

ASK THE CLASS

Ask: How can we comfort Ava’s accuser without attacking Ava? What should we not forward?

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER

“I’m sorry you feel hurt. Let’s talk to someone who can help. We shouldn’t tell everyone not to speak to Ava when we don’t know what happened.” Avoid screenshots being redistributed or public pile-ons.

If bullying, threats or safety concerns are alleged, tell a trusted adult, share accurate evidence privately and do not investigate it alone.

Partners take turns: A makes a claim, B responds calmly in one or two sentences. Invite volunteers to model effective wording.

Independent check

ASK THE CLASS

Students choose a case card and answer in full sentences. Allow pairs to rehearse aloud before independent writing.

EXPECTED DISCUSSION / MODEL ANSWER
