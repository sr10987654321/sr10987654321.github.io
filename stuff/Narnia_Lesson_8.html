Part 2 of 5 · Landscape

A wild and varied land

Narnia's landscape is a mix of rolling, rugged hills and low mountains. It is formed from predominantly dense forest, with the exception of the flooded wetlands to the north.

Geographically, the country centres around the Great River of Narnia and is bordered to the west by an uninhabited mountain range known as the Western Wild and the River Shribble, with the country of Archenland to the south.

Which details would help us draw a map of Narnia?

Reread the model text
Part 3 of 5 · Celebrations

Feasting at Cair Paravel

Narnians take pride in hosting lavish, celebratory feasts. The one true king of Narnia, Aslan, hosts such banquets to celebrate the arrival of human visitors. Interestingly, the celebration at Cair Paravel saw the dining hall ceremoniously decorated with candelabras, garlands and nature-inspired décor.

Furthermore, the foods served included soups, venison, pies, toffees, Turkish Delight and many kinds of tea. Following dinner, attendees could partake in a traditional game called 'Hunt the Slipper'.

What impression do these details give us of the Narnians?

Part 4 of 5 · Wildlife

Ordinary and extraordinary creatures

Narnia is well known for its abundant wildlife. A vast majority of animals commonly found in England are also located in Narnia, such as badgers, beavers and foxes.

Additionally, you may be lucky enough to spot a gryphon or even a minotaur in the snow-covered forests. Furthermore, there is nothing quite like experiencing the wonder of watching a rare phoenix fly across a moonlit sky.

Why might the writer include both familiar and mythical animals?

Part 5 of 5 · Conclusion

A secret of Narnian time

A trip to Narnia typically lasts longer than the corresponding period of absence from Earth, and visitors to Narnia will experience this incredible phenomenon.

As a result, your Earth friends and family may not even notice that you have disappeared into a wardrobe!

Narnia · 11
Think it
Inference round

Join clues with your own thinking.

1
Why might the writer describe the decorations and food in detail?
To help the reader picture the feast and understand how lavish it is.
2
Why does Narnia contain both ordinary and mythical animals?
The ordinary animals make it familiar, while the mythical animals make it magical.
3
What clues show that Narnia is a magical place?
Witches, wardrobes, gryphons, minotaurs, a phoenix and unusual time.
4
Why might people on Earth not realise someone visited Narnia?
Narnian time passes differently, so almost no time may pass on Earth.
5
What does the feast suggest about the Narnians?
They seem welcoming, generous, celebratory and proud of their traditions.
6
Why are the forests described as “dense”?
To show that the trees grow thickly and the forest may be difficult to travel through.
With a partner: orally rehearse two answers. Include a clue and explain it.
