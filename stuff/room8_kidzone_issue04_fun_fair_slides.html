The story

Everything was going brilliantly… until it wasn’t.

Mr Richmondson had organised the biggest Room 8 Fun Fair in school history. There were prizes, popcorn, tickets and balloons. The Duckies were in charge of quality control — which mostly meant eating the snacks.

Then the fair’s automatic control box flashed red. The ticket scanner stopped. The prize counter froze. Even the balloon pump went silent.

The problem

THE FAIR IS LOCKED!

A note appeared on the control box:

“To restart the Fun Fair, enter the five-digit code. Each digit is hidden inside a maths mission.”

Mr Richmondson looked at the Duckies. The Duckies looked at the snacks. Nobody had a clue.

Your mission

Can Room 8 save the Fun Fair?

1. Solve the five puzzles on your worksheets.

2. Show your working clearly — some problems need three or four steps.

3. Take the ones digit of each final answer, in order.

4. Enter the five digits into the keypad.

Work independently or with your assigned partner. Check whether each answer makes sense.

A quick reminder

Which digit do we keep?

If your final answer is 347, the ones digit is 7.

If your final answer is 520, the ones digit is 0.

Keep the digits in mission order. Don’t swap them around!

Mission 1 / Question

The Ticket Disaster

Mr Richmondson printed 15 sheets of 24 tickets for the Room 8 Fun Fair. Mrs Zeading found another 186 tickets in a drawer, but 73 tickets were damaged by a leaking drink. How many usable tickets are there?
Mission 1 / Worked answer

The Ticket Disaster

15 × 24 = 360
360 + 186 = 546
546 − 73 = 473
{ans} → digit {ans%10}
Mission 2 / Question

The Prize Counter

The prize counter started with 864 tokens. They packed the tokens equally into 8 tubs. One tub was opened, and 257 extra tokens were added to its contents. Then 96 tokens were given away. How many tokens were left in that tub?
Mission 2 / Worked answer

The Prize Counter

864 ÷ 8 = 108
108 + 257 = 365
365 − 96 = 269
{ans} → digit {ans%10}
Mission 3 / Question

The Popcorn Panic

There were 23 trays holding 18 popcorn cups each. The Duckies also brought 168 extra cups, shared equally between 6 stalls. One stall received its share of extras, but 97 cups from the original trays were crushed. How many usable cups did that stall have if all 23 trays were delivered there?
Mission 3 / Worked answer

The Popcorn Panic

23 × 18 = 414
168 ÷ 6 = 28
414 + 28 = 442
442 − 97 = 345
{ans} → digit {ans%10}
Mission 4 / Question

The Mystery Money Tin

The Fun Fair money tin held $1,250. Mr Richmondson paid for 16 boxes of supplies at $37 each. Later, a parent donated another $84. How much money was now in the tin?
Mission 4 / Worked answer

The Mystery Money Tin

16 × 37 = 592
1,250 − 592 = 658
658 + 84 = 742
{ans} → digit {ans%10}
Mission 5 / Question

The Final Balloon Count

There were 945 balloons shared equally among 9 classrooms. Room 8 then received 28 packets of 13 extra balloons. During decorating, 176 balloons popped or floated away. How many balloons did Room 8 have left?
Mission 5 / Worked answer

The Final Balloon Count

945 ÷ 9 = 105
28 × 13 = 364
105 + 364 = 469
469 − 176 = 293
{ans} → digit {ans%10}

THE SECRET SNACK STALL

The snack stall made 27 trays of 16 biscuits. They also found 384 spare biscuits and shared them equally between 8 stalls. Room 8 received one share, but sold 159 biscuits in total. How many biscuits did Room 8 have left?

Show all four calculations. What would the ones digit be?

The Secret Snack Stall

27 × 16 = 432
384 ÷ 8 = 48
432 + 48 = 480
480 − 159 = 321
