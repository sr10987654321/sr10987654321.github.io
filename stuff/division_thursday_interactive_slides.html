DIVISION STRATEGY CHAMPIONSHIPS

Today isn't about being the fastest. It's about choosing the smartest strategy — and proving it works.

90 minutes
3 stations
1 mystery

0–3 min. Announce challenge and ensure students have grid books and pencils.

Learning intention

CHOOSE. SOLVE. PROVE.

Choose

I can decide which division strategy will help me.

Solve

I can show each step clearly in my grid book.

Prove

I can multiply to check my answer and explain my thinking.

3–5 min. Ask students to recall known strategies: think multiplication, halve, partition, and large jumps.

Warm-up · 5 minutes

CHOOSE YOUR WEAPON

Which strategy would you use? Tell your partner why before you calculate.

5–10 min. Cold-call explanations. 63 ÷ 7 encourages think multiplication; 84 ÷ 4 halving twice or partition; 144 ÷ 6 friendly split.

Strategy showdown · 10 minutes

156 ÷ 3

Same answer. Two very different-looking methods.

Partition

150 ÷ 3 = 50

6 ÷ 3 = 2

50 + 2 = 52

Large jumps

10 groups = 30

40 groups = 120

2 groups = 6

10 + 40 + 2 = 52

Check

What multiplication proves the result?

52 × 3 = 156 ✓

10–20 min. Work through the 156 ÷ 3 example. Ask students how the two representations are connected.

Interactive modelling

BREAK IT INTO FRIENDLY PARTS

Change the numbers. Choose a partition that divides exactly.

Live model. Try 168 ÷ 6 using 120 + 48 and 216 ÷ 4 using 200 + 16; also deliberately try an unhelpful partition to discuss why.

Station instructions · 45 minutes

THREE STATIONS.
FIFTEEN MINUTES EACH.

01 / Partition Masters

Break the number into friendly parts.

02 / Giant Jumpers

Represent division with large groups.

03 / Error Detectives

Spot and explain someone else's mistake.

Everyone records their own working. Solve, check and explain. Don't just copy your partner's answer.

20–65 min. Organise 3 rotation groups. Each station has its own projected screen and can be revisited; teacher can also print this deck. Students need grid books and station task instructions.

Station 1 · Partition masters

BREAK IT TO MAKE IT

Solve using friendly-number partitioning. Click a question to check.

Grid book: number → partition → two divisions → combined answer → multiplication check.

Core: 124 ÷ 4, 168 ÷ 6, 225 ÷ 5, 288 ÷ 8. Extension: find THREE valid partitions for 288 ÷ 8. Suggested 240+48, 160+128, 200+88.

Station 2 · Giant jumpers

BIG JUMPS, SMART THINKING

Build an open number line. Use chunks like 10 groups, 20 groups or 5 groups — then add your groups.

Grid book: labelled number line → total number of groups → multiplication check.

Core: 132 ÷ 6, 192 ÷ 8, 245 ÷ 7, 324 ÷ 9. Extension: 432 ÷ 6 using two efficient jumps (60 groups + 12 groups).

Station 3 · Error detectives

FIND THE MISTAKE

Don't just correct the answer. Explain exactly where the thinking went wrong.

Case A

96 ÷ 4

80 ÷ 4 = 20

16 ÷ 4 = 9

Answer = 29

16 ÷ 4 = 4, not 9.
20 + 4 = 24.

Case B

144 ÷ 6

120 ÷ 6 = 20

24 ÷ 6 = 4

Answer = 16

The parts should be ADDED. 20 + 4 = 24, not 16.

Case C

168 ÷ 4

160 ÷ 4 = 40

8 ÷ 4 = 2

Answer = 42

No mistake! 42 × 4 = 168. Not every worked example is wrong.

Require a sentence for each: 'The mistake was ... because ...'. Extension: create a believable wrong example for another student to diagnose.

Station timer

15:00

Finish recording your reasoning before you rotate.

Leave this projected while stations are running if useful. Timer starts only when clicked and runs locally in browser.

Final investigation · 18 minutes

THE MYSTERY NUMBER

I am a three-digit number.
I am greater than 100 but smaller than 300.
I can be divided exactly by 3, 4 and 6.
What numbers could I be?

Find every possibility. Prove it works. Prove you haven't missed any.

Look for a number that 3, 4 and 6 all divide into. Start with multiples of 12.

65–83 min. This is an open-ended task, with systematic recording: candidate number, divisibility tests, multiplication proof, then completeness argument.

Mystery · Extension

CAN YOU NARROW IT DOWN?

New clue: my mystery number is also divisible by 5.

Which possibilities remain? Explain the pattern.

Original possibilities: 108, 120, 132, 144, 156, 168, 180, 192, 204, 216, 228, 240, 252, 264, 276, 288.

With ÷ 5 added: 120, 180, 240.

For fast finishers: create clues that isolate a single answer, or change the upper/lower bounds and predict how many valid numbers exist.

Reflection · 7 minutes

WHAT'S YOUR GO-TO STRATEGY?

Finish this sentence in your grid book:

When I divide large numbers, I like to ______ because ______.

EXIT TICKET

216 ÷ 4 = ?

Solve it with a strategy and prove it with multiplication.

216 = 200 + 16 → 200 ÷ 4 = 50, 16 ÷ 4 = 4 → 54.
Check: 54 × 4 = 216 ✓

83–90 min. Collect exit tickets to check strategy choice, working clarity and verification. Provide manipulatives or smaller numbers next lesson for pupils who need additional scaffolding.
