Relief Notes: Does Chance Have a Memory?

YEAR 4 MATHEMATICS50–60 MINUTESCHANCE · LESSON 2
The main idea: A fair coin, die or unchanged spinner begins each trial with the same chances. Earlier results do not make another outcome “due”. Chances can change when the setup changes, such as when a counter is removed and not returned.

Before the lesson

  • Open lesson_2_chance_has_no_memory_slides.html and enter full screen.
  • Have one coin per pair. A two-sided counter marked H and T also works.
  • Students need maths books, pencils and rulers.
  • The information button contains extra notes, but the teaching directions also appear on each slide.

Learning intention

We are learning whether what happened before changes what could happen next.

Students should be able to: explain why a fair coin stays 50–50 and identify when removing an item changes the next chance event.

Lesson sequence

What students produce

  • A numbered sequence of 20 coin-toss results.
  • Streaks of three or more circled.
  • Their longest streak identified.
  • A sentence explaining whether a streak changes the next toss.
  • Three exit-ticket answers.

Answers and Expected Work

Essential explanations

After five heads: Heads and tails are still equally likely. The coin has not changed and cannot remember the earlier results. Every fair toss begins again with the same two outcomes.
Putting a counter back: The bag returns to its original contents, so the chances stay the same. Keeping it out: The contents change, so the chances may change.

Slide 7 answers

  1. Fair die rolled again: SAME
  2. Red counter drawn and put back: SAME
  3. Blue counter drawn and kept out: CHANGED
  4. Card drawn and returned: SAME
  5. Card drawn and kept out: CHANGED

Expected investigation

RESULTS H, T, T, T, H, H, T, H, H, H, H, T, T, H, T, H, H, T, T, H

LONGEST STREAK Four heads

“I found several streaks. The coin was still fair because every new toss had an equal chance of heads or tails.”

Student sequences and streak lengths will vary. They meet the expectation when they record 20 trials and explain that a streak does not control the next fair toss.

Exit-ticket answers

  1. Heads and tails are equally likely.
  2. Each fair toss is a new chance event. The coin has not changed, so its earlier results do not affect the next toss.
  3. Yes. Keeping the blue counter out changes the contents of the bag. There is now one fewer blue counter available.

Common misconceptions

Student saysUseful response
“Tails has to come next.”Ask: “What has physically changed about the coin?” Nothing has changed, so the next toss remains 50–50.
“Six heads is impossible.”Six heads is unlikely, but it can happen. Impossible means it cannot happen at all.
“We should get exactly ten of each.”Equal chances do not guarantee equal totals in only 20 tosses. Random results vary.
“The chances always stay the same.”Ask whether the collection or equipment changed. Removing an item can change the chances.

If time is short

Complete Slides 1–7, reduce the investigation to 10 tosses, show the expected-work slide and finish with the exit ticket. The exit ticket is the most useful individual assessment, so keep it if possible.

If students finish early

Students toss another 20 times and compare the two sets. They write whether the totals and longest streak stayed the same, then explain why variation is expected.
