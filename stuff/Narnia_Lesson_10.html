Narnia · 03
A hidden boundary
The first paragraph does two jobs

Sections and paragraphs do not always match.

Box 1 · Introduction

The Kingdom of Narnia is a fantasy world home to lions, witches and wardrobes.

This sentence introduces the subject.

Box 2 · Landscape

Narnia's landscape is a mix of rolling rugged hills and low mountains...

The rest of the paragraph describes the land and its position.

Box by the job each part performs, even when two boxes sit inside one paragraph.

Narnia · 07
Box 2 · Body 1

Landscape

Model text

Narnia's landscape is a mix of rolling rugged hills and low mountains. It is formed from predominantly dense forest with the exception of the flooded wetlands to the north.

Geographically, the country centres around the Great River of Narnia and bordered on the west by an uninhabited mountain range known as The Western Wild and the River Shribble, with the country of Archenland to the south.

Boxed-up notes

  • Rolling hills and low mountains
  • Dense forest
  • Wetlands north
  • Great River through centre
  • Western Wild and River Shribble west
  • Archenland south
Keep the direction words. They hold the geography together.
Narnia · 08
Box 3 · Body 2

Celebrations

Model text

Narnians take pride in hosting lavish, celebratory feasts. The one true king of Narnia, Aslan, hosts such banquets to celebrate the arrival of human visitors. Interestingly, the celebration at Cair Paravel, saw the dining hall ceremoniously decorated with candelabras, garlands and nature-inspired décor.

Furthermore, the foods served included soups, venison, pies, toffees, Turkish Delight, and many kinds of tea. Following dinner, attendees could partake in a traditional game called ‘Hunt the Slipper’.

Boxed-up notes

  • Lavish feasts
  • Aslan welcomes human visitors
  • Cair Paravel decorated
  • Soups, venison, pies, sweets and tea
  • Hunt the Slipper after dinner
Group long lists when the individual items are not all needed for the plan.
Narnia · 09
Box 4 · Body 3

Wildlife

Model text

Narnia is well known for its abundant wildlife. A vast majority of animals commonly found in England are also located in Narnia, such as badgers, beavers and foxes.

Additionally, you may be lucky enough to spot a gryphon or even a minotaur in the snow-covered forests. Furthermore, there is nothing quite like experiencing the wonder of watching a rare phoenix fly across a moonlit sky.

Boxed-up notes

  • Abundant wildlife
  • Badgers, beavers and foxes
  • Gryphons and minotaurs in forest
  • Rare phoenix
The notes preserve the paragraph's main pattern: familiar animals and mythical creatures.
Narnia · 10
Box 5 · Conclusion

Narnian time

Model text

A trip to Narnia typically lasts longer than the corresponding period of their absence from Earth, and visitors to Narnia will experience this incredible phenomenon.

As a result, your Earth friends and family may not even notice that you have disappeared into a wardrobe!

Boxed-up notes

  • Narnian visit feels longer
  • Very little time passes on Earth
  • Family may not notice
A conclusion can finish with a memorable fact rather than repeat every section.

Independent task
Box up the model

Your Narnia report map

Complete the five boxes

  1. Write the section name and its job.
  2. Add the essential facts as short dot points.
  3. Use the model text to check that nothing important is missing.

Keep it brief

Use words and phrases, not copied sentences.

Your notes should help you say the report aloud in your own words.

Challenge: write no more than six dot points in any box.
