Today’s learning

Data can inform a prediction

We are learning to

collect results, graph them and use the results to make a sensible prediction.

By the end, you can

compare colour frequencies and explain why a prediction may differ from the actual result.

READ TOGETHER: A prediction is what we think may happen. It is not a guarantee.

Part 1 · Your box

Count every colour

Open your box carefully and keep every Smartie on your desk.

Sort the Smarties into colour groups.

Record the number of each colour in a table.

Check that your colour totals equal the total number of Smarties.

IMPORTANT: Do not eat any Smarties yet. Record a zero if a colour is missing.

Part 2 · Groups of four

Combine your results

Put four individual data tables together.

Add the four blue counts. Repeat for every colour.

Check that the combined colour total is 44 Smarties.

Create a new graph using the combined results.

TEAM CHECK: Every person should agree with every total before graphing.

Predict the next box

The explanation

Why did the new box differ?

Our group data helped us make a sensible prediction. Why did it not tell us the exact contents?

Each box can vary.

A more common colour may be more likely, but it does not have to appear a particular number of times in one small box.

THINK, PAIR, SHARE: Explain first. Then click to reveal the key idea.
