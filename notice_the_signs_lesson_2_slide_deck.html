Notice the Signs

How can we notice what a friend might need, then choose a thoughtful next step?

Lesson 2: Friendship Chalk Walk

Last lesson

Small choices can affect how brightly someone feels.

Today we are practising how to notice those moments earlier.

Learning intention

We are learning to notice clues in people’s words, faces and behaviour so we can respond thoughtfully.

Success criteria

1

Notice

What clues can I see or hear?

2

Think

What might they be feeling or needing?

3

Choose

What could I say or do next?

Big idea

Good friends do not have to guess perfectly.

They notice, check and listen.

Teacher quick act

Someone says, “I’m fine,” but they look down, pack roughly, and stop joining in.

Students: What clues did you notice?

Be careful

We can notice clues, but we cannot read minds.

A thoughtful friend checks instead of assuming.

Friendship Chalk Walk

Around the undercover area, each button has a scenario recorded on it.

Press. Listen. Think. Chalk.

At each button, write:

Notice Feel Say Do

You can write words, short phrases, arrows or quick sketches.

Example

Scenario

Your friend is usually chatty, but today they are very quiet.

Chalk ideas

Notice: quiet, looking down
Feel: sad, tired, worried
Say: “Do you want to talk or have space?”
Do: sit nearby, don’t push

Chalk Walk rules

  • Move calmly between buttons.
  • Read other groups’ ideas before adding yours.
  • Add a new idea, circle a strong one, or draw an arrow to connect ideas.
  • No real names. Use “someone,” “a friend,” or “the group.”

Work time

25 minutes

In pairs or small groups, visit as many buttons as you can.

Gallery walk

Walk around and read the chalk clouds.

Find one response that is thoughtful, realistic or surprising.

Share

What was one strong response you saw?

Explain why it would help in a friendship.

Exit question

What is one clue that someone might need us to respond differently?

Write your answer on a sticky note or say it to your partner.

Final thought

A thoughtful friend notices, checks and listens.
